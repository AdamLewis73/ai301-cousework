## Rules I write by

<!-- 3-5 rules, drafted from the lecture's slide-12 moment. Each rule

Rule: no unnecessary compliments
- Wrong: "Wow amazing job"

\- Right: "I will try to pick this up from here"



Rule: no unnecessary comparisons ("it's not X, it's Y")

\- Wrong: "You aren't just seeing a bug, you a seeing a systematic breakdown across the codebase"

\- Right: "This bug is fairly significant"



Rule: clearly state versions and impact

\- Wrong: "This bug sucks"

\- Right: "The commit hash bdfcd43c153e13ad041eb734da4d79dd82ab08e6 bug prevents user login"



Rule: no promised sel-imposed deadlines

\- Wrong: "I will finish this by Friday."

\- Right: "I will work on this immediately."



## Things I never post

<!-- A short list. Promises you cannot keep, tones you refuse,

shortcuts you know you reach for when tired. The skill quotes this

list back at you when a draft crosses it. -->

I never make definitive answers about something I don't have expertise in. ("I know exactly why this language I've never programmed in isn't working")
I never post in a tone that is not professional.
I don't promise results, I show them.
